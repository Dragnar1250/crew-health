                        Sources and limits of this prototype

                        NASA

                        • NASA Human Research Program, 5 Hazards of Human Spaceflight: the hazard model behind every body region.
                        • NASA, The Human Body in Space: bone loss of 1% to 1.5% per month, one-way signal delay of up to 20 minutes from Mars.
                        • NASA OCHMO-TB-004, Carbon Dioxide (NASA-STD-3001 [V2 6004]): cabin limit of 3 mmHg as a 1-hour average, symptoms reported from 2.8 to 4.5 mmHg.
                        • NASA OCHMO Radiation Protection technical brief: 600 mSv career limit, 250 mSv per solar particle event.
                        • NASA, Space Faring: The Radiation Challenge: about 80 mSv (solar maximum) to 160 mSv (solar minimum) for a six-month ISS stay, so 0.66 mSv per day is used for the ISS; about 2 mSv per year from natural background on Earth; sievert, gray and rem.
                        • NASA JPL, Curiosity RAD radiation measurements: 0.64 mSv per day on the Mars surface (Hassler et al., Science, 2014) and 1.84 mSv per day during the cruise to Mars (Zeitlin et al., Science, 2013), as reported by Space.com.
                        • NASA OCHMO-TB-031, Exercise Overview: station crews average about 600 minutes of exercise per week, with 60 to 90 minutes prescribed per day.
                        • NASA OCHMO Sleep Accommodations technical brief: adults need 7 to 9 hours of sleep; ISS crews average 6 to 6.5 hours.
                        • NASA OCHMO Water technical brief (NASA-STD-3001 [V2 6109]): at least 2.5 L of drinking water per crew member per day; losing water equal to 2.5% of body weight lowers concentration and physical performance.
                        • NASA, Risk of performance decrements from sleep loss, circadian desynchronization and work overload: the risk behind the performance score.
                        • NASA OCHMO-TB-006, Pharmaceuticals: NASA-STD-3001 [V2 7059] requires tracking item locations and quantities; the ISS kit is split into packs and holds 190 medications; average expiration is 1 to 2 years while exploration missions are planned for 1 to 5 years; NASA tested a Dose Tracker app for logging medication use. The example medicines come from the formulary items it names.

                        Other spaceflight research

                        • Zhang et al., First measurements of the radiation dose on the lunar surface, Science Advances, 2020: 1.369 mSv per day on the lunar surface (Chang'e 4), against 0.731 mSv per day inside the ISS in the same period.
                        • Optic nerve sheath diameter and spaceflight, npj Microgravity, 2022: no single cut-off exists (5.0 to 6.4 mm on Earth) and astronaut values without eye changes average about 5.8 mm, so the personal preflight value matters most.

                        Clinical reference ranges

                        • American Heart Association, Understanding Blood Pressure Readings: normal below 120/80 mmHg, crisis above 180/120 mmHg.
                        • Mayo Clinic, What's a normal resting heart rate?: 60 to 100 bpm for adults, close to 40 bpm for very fit athletes.
                        • MedlinePlus Magazine, Pulse oximeters: 95% to 100% for most healthy people, accuracy limits of finger sensors.
                        • MedlinePlus, Blood gases: PaCO2 38 to 42 mmHg at sea level; ranges vary between labs.
                        • American Diabetes Association, Classification of Hypoglycemia (Diabetes Care, 2017): level 1 below 70 mg/dL, level 2 below 54 mg/dL.
                        • Jones et al., Sleep deficiency in spaceflight is associated with degraded neurobehavioral functions and elevated stress in astronauts on six-month missions aboard the ISS, Sleep, 2022: the ISS Reaction Self Test uses a 3-minute psychomotor vigilance test; a response of 355 ms or more is a lapse; sleep of 6 hours or less slowed responses.
                        • Back Pain in Outer Space, Anesthesiology, 2021: up to 52% of astronauts report back pain, mostly in the first 5 days; more than 90% reported relief from the knees-to-chest position.
                        • Space.com, ISS LED lighting: general, blue-enriched and pre-sleep light settings on the ISS.
                        • MedlinePlus, Vital signs: a breathing rate of 12 to 18 breaths per minute for a healthy adult at rest.
                        • CDC, Definitions of Signs, Symptoms, and Conditions of Ill Travelers: fever is a measured 100.4 °F (38 °C) or higher.

                        Team decisions and limits

                        • Where a clinical source gives a normal range, this tool uses it for "In range". The "Act now" steps (for example 38.5 °C or a heart rate above 120 bpm) are the team's own proposal for when to call the flight surgeon. A flight version would use each crew member's preflight baseline and NASA-STD-3001 limits.
                        • Cerebrospinal fluid pressure is not measured directly. Optic nerve sheath diameter from eye ultrasound is used as an indirect sign.
                        • Cumulative dose warning steps at 300 mSv (half of the career limit) and 480 mSv (80%) were chosen by the team. The 600 mSv career limit is NASA's.
                        • The weekly morale survey and its General Psychological Risk Index were designed by the team. It is not a validated clinical instrument, and its thresholds are a first proposal.
                        • The medical kit is an example. Amounts, strengths and dates are invented for the demo, and the tool does not give medicine doses.
                        • The analysis rules (which readings together point to a problem, the possible causes and the steps) and the extreme situation checks are the team's own first-response summaries. They do not replace official flight rules and medical checklists.
                        • The combined check compares each reading with the crew member's own mean and spread and tests the sum of squared distances with a chi-square distribution. It treats the readings as independent, so it is a screening signal. A flight version would use the full correlation between readings.
                        • Symptoms are self-rated from 0 (none) to 3 (severe), and stress from 0 to 10. These scales were chosen by the team.
                        • The performance risk score, its weights and its forecast, the targeted advice, the light plan windows and the reaction test thresholds for "Watch" and "Act now" are the team's own proposal.
                        • Live telemetry and sleep stages are simulated. A flight version would read the crew member's wearable sensors.
                        • Reports are simulated. Nothing is transmitted. This is not a medical device.
